Man-shaped help now available by the hour

Two hundred bipeds stand ready in six depots. They do not tire, gossip, or ask for the aux cable. They do, however, need charging.

Rotterdam — For the first time, a household, a warehouse or a wedding planner may hire a full-sized humanoid the way one hires a van: by the hour, delivered to the door, and collected when the work is done.

"People expect a robot arm on a rail," said a depot supervisor, who asked to be identified only by her shift. "Then a thing in a hi-vis vest walks up the drive and asks where the boxes go. It changes the mood."

Each unit is supervised remotely by a licensed operator, who can be reached by telephone at any hour. Damage is insured. Ladders, the company stresses, are not permitted.

Rates begin at nineteen euros an hour for the Queuer model, which stands in lines on behalf of the public. Demand, the company reports, has been "embarrassingly high."
